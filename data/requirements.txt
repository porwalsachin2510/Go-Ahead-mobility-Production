1. Executive Summary and Product Goals
GoAhead requires a modern, responsive and premium-quality corporate website that presents two related but distinct business verticals: Global Accommodation and Local Transportation. The experience should build trust with enterprise decision-makers, explain service capabilities, capture qualified enquiries, and give authorized staff the ability to manage website content, SEO, editorial publishing and leads through a secure admin panel.
The application is to be built in two phases. Phase 1 is a corporate marketing and lead-generation platform. Phase 2 extends the same product foundation into a B2B travel booking platform where approved corporate users sign in, search bookable inventory, request approvals, book hotels and flights, and manage fulfilment, payments/invoicing and reporting. Phase 2 requires supplier/GDS/NDC/aggregator contracts and APIs; it cannot be delivered reliably by scraping consumer booking sites.
1.1 Business Outcomes
Explain the Global Accommodation proposition with clear service details, coverage, procurement benefits and dedicated accommodation enquiry forms.
Explain Local Transportation services: corporate cab, employee transport, shuttle, industrial/workforce, event transport and transport software/ERP.
Convert high-intent B2B visitors into traceable leads with source attribution, internal ownership, SLA reminders and email notifications.
Allow non-technical users to safely manage pages, navigation, banners, case studies, FAQs, service areas, blog posts, SEO metadata and leads.
Provide a search-friendly, accessible and performance-focused public site with a clear content hierarchy and maintainable page templates.
Create a secure technical foundation that supports later company accounts, traveller profiles, travel policies, approval workflows, bookings, supplier APIs and finance operations.
1.2 Definition of Success
Area
How success will be measured
Lead generation
Form completion rate, qualified lead volume by vertical, lead response time, sales follow-up completion and proposal conversion.
Content operations
Authorized admins can publish, preview, schedule, update and roll back pages/blog posts without developer involvement.
SEO health
Indexable priority pages, correct canonical URLs, crawlable sitemap, valid schema where eligible, no critical broken links and Search Console monitoring.
Experience
Usable layouts on mobile/tablet/desktop, accessible forms and menus, responsive interaction, fast loading and clear conversion actions.

Stable organization/user identity model, modular APIs, versioned contracts and a documented integration boundary for inventory providers.
SEO promise clarification
No system can guarantee the #1 position on every search engine. The system must enable best-practice technical SEO, useful original content, structured data, measurement and continuous improvement. Rankings depend on competition, query intent, domain authority, content quality, market changes and search engine decisions.

2.2 Migration Rules
Create a full URL inventory before changing slugs. For every old URL, record new target, status code, canonical target and index/noindex treatment.
Prefer preserving proven URLs, including underscores currently present, unless an intentional URL cleanup is approved.
Use server-side 301 redirects for permanently moved pages. Avoid redirect chains, redirect loops and broad redirects to the homepage.
Migrate existing copy and media only after verifying factual accuracy, rights, current product availability and brand approval.
Preserve search-relevant page titles, headings, image context and useful internal links where appropriate; improve rather than blindly copy weak content.
Create a pre-launch crawl and a post-launch crawl, plus Search Console monitoring for 404s, indexation changes and redirect issues.

3.1 Phase 1 Scope
Public corporate website with dual-vertical homepage, complete sitemap and mobile-responsive navigation.
Dedicated Global Accommodation and Local Transportation landing pages and service-detail pages.
About/company pages, industries pages, locations/coverage, trust/safety, commercial model, contact and careers pages.

3.2 Phase 2 Scope
Company/tenant accounts, user and traveller profiles, corporate hierarchy, travel policies and multi-level approval workflows.
Hotel and flight search and bookings via licensed supplier, aggregator, GDS, NDC or API integrations; clear inventory/provider attribution.
Bookings, holds where supported, booking confirmation, PNR/ticketing where supported, vouchers, changes, cancellations, refunds and customer support.
Company billing, credit/limit management, invoices, payment reconciliation, cost centres, budgets, finance reports and audit trails.
Admin, operations, finance, supplier and customer portals with strict permission separation.
Future additions such as airport transfer/corporate cab reservations, rail, bus, car rental, insurance and travel add-ons through an extensible product connector model.

6.4 Form Engineering and Data Handling
Client-side validation for immediate guidance plus authoritative server-side validation for every field and file.
Normalize email addresses, phone numbers, dates, country codes and enum values; store dates in UTC and preserve the entered local time zone when relevant.
Protect from duplicate submissions using idempotency token/submission key; show a helpful retry state without losing filled form data.
Create the lead record first, then queue/send emails; email failure must not discard a saved lead. Log delivery status and allow resend by an authorized admin.
Use rate limits, bot detection/honeypot and CAPTCHA only where justified; avoid accessibility-hostile challenges.
Limit file type/size, verify MIME and signature, rename stored assets, virus-scan where available, store non-public RFP files privately, and log every authorized download.
Never expose MongoDB IDs or internal stack traces in public responses; return a public enquiry reference number.
After success, show acknowledgement with next step; do not show a made-up SLA or assert a booking is confirmed.
Use server-side attribution capture: landing page, referrer, UTM source/medium/campaign/content/term, timestamp, consent, form version, optional campaign click IDs where legal.

11.2 Next.js Application: UI and Rendering
Use Next.js App Router with TypeScript as the sole web application framework. Build the interface with React components inside Next.js; do not create or deploy a separate React SPA/frontend or maintain a separate frontend repository.
Use Next.js route groups for public content, admin and the future authenticated customer portal. Keep protected pages and all sensitive data access server-authorized; route grouping is not a substitute for authorization.
Use a shared design system, accessible forms, responsive layouts and image/video optimization. Keep public-route client bundles lean and load admin-only UI code only where required.
Use typed request/response schemas, a centralized API access pattern where needed, error boundaries, loading/empty/error states and safe environment configuration. Server components should access server-only modules directly where appropriate instead of making unnecessary HTTP requests back into the same application.
Primary SEO HTML must include key copy and metadata from Next.js server rendering or static generation before client-side hydration; avoid client-only shells for important organic landing pages.
11.3 Node.js Backend within the Next.js Application
Use a supported Node.js LTS runtime for server-side logic within the same Next.js project and deployment. Organize auth, CMS, leads, SEO, media, blog, notifications, analytics and future booking as isolated server-only modules; do not build a separate Express/NestJS backend service for core website functionality.
Implement the versioned REST API (suggested prefix /api/v1) with Next.js Route Handlers. Apply request validation, centralized error handling, structured logging, rate limits and authentication/authorization guards to every applicable endpoint.
Maintain an OpenAPI/Swagger contract for API documentation; use consistent pagination/filter/sort rules, typed request/response contracts, validation and idempotency for submission endpoints. Keep API contracts versioned even though UI and API live in one codebase.
Use a durable background queue and scheduled processing for email, large exports, image-processing callbacks, sitemap generation and future supplier sync. Do not depend on unawaited in-memory tasks inside a web request for critical work; a dedicated worker process may be used only when the queue/deployment model requires it, not as a separate core API backend.
Use an outbox/retry pattern where a saved lead must trigger external notifications reliably; store delivery attempts and dead-letter failures independently of the web request lifecycle.
11.4 Database and Infrastructure
MongoDB Atlas with network access restrictions, least-privilege database users, automatic backups, point-in-time recovery where supported and tested restore procedures.
Mongoose or official MongoDB driver with schema validation and explicit indexes; use transactions when a multi-document workflow truly requires atomicity and deployment tier supports them.
Separate development, staging and production environments with distinct secrets, database clusters/credentials and Cloudinary/email configurations.
Infrastructure-as-code is recommended. Keep secrets in a managed secret store/environment config, never in source control, client bundles or public NEXT_PUBLIC_ environment variables.
Use a CDN/WAF and TLS; application logs, uptime checks, error monitoring, database health and backup alerts.
11.5 Application Boundary and Module Evolution
Start with one Next.js full-stack modular monolith for Phase 1: one source repository and one application deployment containing the UI, Node.js server-side logic and API routes. Keep module boundaries and provider interfaces clean so booking orchestration, supplier connectors, finance or messaging can be extracted into separate services later only if proven scale, team structure or operational needs justify it. Do not introduce a separate React frontend or standalone API backend for Phase 1, and do not start with unnecessary microservices or distributed transactions.

14. Security, Privacy, Reliability and Operations
14.1 Identity and Application Security
Strong password hashing via a current, battle-tested algorithm or use a trusted identity provider; never store plaintext passwords or send passwords by email.
MFA mandatory for Super Admin and recommended for other privileged users; secure reset flows, short-lived reset tokens and session invalidation after credential change.
Brute-force protection, rate limits, lockout/backoff, suspicious login alerting and admin session timeout appropriate to role/risk.
RBAC enforced on backend for every route and object; test horizontal/vertical privilege escalation, tenant/record leakage and IDOR patterns.
Use TLS, secure and HttpOnly cookies if cookie sessions are selected, SameSite policy, CSRF defense where needed, strict CORS, CSP/security headers and dependency scanning.
Prevent XSS by sanitizing rich text and rendering safe blocks; prevent injection with validated queries and safe database APIs; protect against SSRF by disallowing arbitrary server-side fetches from user-controlled URLs.
Secrets stored in managed secret store/environment; rotate API keys; restrict Cloudinary/email/database permissions; no production secret in browser bundles or source control.
Validate all uploads and webhooks. Limit request body size and reject unexpected content types.
14.2 Privacy and Data Governance
Publish a privacy notice that accurately covers business enquiries, application data, analytics/cookies, email/newsletter consent, CRM exports, retention and data subject contact route.
Collect only data needed for the current purpose. Public Phase 1 enquiry forms must not request passports, payment card data, health details or other unnecessary sensitive information.
Marketing consent is separate, optional and unselected by default; log wording/version/time/source and provide unsubscribe/suppression logic.
Define retention periods for leads, resumes, email logs, audit logs, inactive users, failed jobs and temporary files with legal/client approval.
Provide authorized workflows for data access/correction/deletion requests and legal holds. Ensure backup retention and deletion exceptions are documented.
Use consent management for non-essential cookies/analytics where required; do not fire optional tracking before consent when the chosen legal regime requires opt-in.
Phase 2 traveller profile, passport, payment and itinerary data require data minimization, stronger access controls, retention rules and incident response; payment card details should be tokenized by a compliant provider rather than stored in MongoDB.

17. Phase 2 Booking Architecture, Data Model and Operations
17.1 Provider Connector Architecture
The booking engine must isolate provider-specific behavior behind adapters. Each adapter defines search, price recheck, book/hold (if supported), retrieve, cancel, change/refund/ticketing, policies, timeout/retry behavior, rate limits, credential scope, mapping and reconciliation. Provider capabilities should be explicitly declared; unsupported operations should be disabled or routed to operations support.
Never scrape Booking.com, MakeMyTrip or airline public sites to create a commercial booking engine. Use documented APIs, authorized wholesalers/aggregators, GDS/NDC providers or direct hotel contracts.
Normalize supplier-specific status and error codes to a documented internal model while retaining raw provider reference and safe diagnostic context.
Use circuit breakers and bounded retries for safe read calls; booking, ticketing, cancellation and payment operations require idempotency and reconciliation before retry.
Persist the exact quoted offer/price/terms snapshot, supplier, currency, expiry, policy result and approval evidence relevant to each booking.
Use provider webhooks/polling only where contractually supported, with signature validation, replay protection, event deduplication and reconciliation jobs.
